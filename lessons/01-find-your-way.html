Lesson 1: Find your way around

About 12 minutes · Blender 5.2 LTS · Mac Mini

Goal: Move the camera around the default cube, select it, and save a file.

Before you start

Open Blender. A default scene appears with a gray cube, a light, and a camera. Leave them alone for now. Blender's main area is called the 3D Viewport. This is where you build.

Steps

  1. Move the view. Hold the middle mouse button and drag. On a trackpad, use the two-finger drag shown in Blender's navigation settings (check your setting; trackpad options vary by setup and are unverified for 5.2 on your machine).
  2. Zoom. Scroll, or pinch on a trackpad. Scrolling up or pinching in brings the cube closer.
  3. Pan. Hold Shift and drag with the middle button (or use the two-finger drag combined with Shift).
  4. Use the number keys for fixed views. Press 1 for front, 3 for side, 7 for top. On a Mac keyboard without a numeric keypad, these may need the Numpad emulation setting turned on. If the number keys do nothing, look for that setting in Preferences and treat it as unverified for your setup.
  5. Select the cube. Left-click the cube. An orange outline means it is selected. Click empty space to deselect.
  6. Save your work. Press Cmd + S. Name the file lesson01.blend and save it to a folder you will remember.
Mac tip: Blender uses Cmd where Windows uses Ctrl. Undo is Cmd + Z. Undo is your safety net, so use it freely.

Project: stacked "716" cube block

  1. Select the cube and press Shift + D, then right-click to drop the copy in place. Copies stay in the same spot until you move them. We will move them in Lesson 2.
  2. Repeat once more so you have three cubes. Do not worry about neat placement yet.
  3. Save again with Cmd + S.
